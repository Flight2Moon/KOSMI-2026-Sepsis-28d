Study overview

Abstract

Prognostic models in sepsis have focused predominantly on mortality, yet survivors follow divergent hospital courses. A clinically intuitive alternative to a single multiclass model is a death-first cascade, but whether this added structure improves prediction is unclear. We conducted a retrospective multi-database cohort study of adults meeting Sepsis-3 criteria. Models were developed on MIMIC-IV (development subset 2008-2016, n=17,009) using 56 routinely available variables from the first 24 hours after ICU admission, and evaluated on a temporal split (2017-2022) and, after refitting on the full MIMIC-IV cohort, on four external cohorts: two prespecified eICU-CRD cohort definitions, SICdb, and NWICU. The outcome comprised three mutually exclusive hospital states 28 days after hospital admission: early discharge, in-hospital death, and prolonged hospitalization. Five algorithms were each fitted as a flat three-class model and as a death-first cascade. Macro AUROC was the primary metric; differences were assessed by paired bootstrap resampling with 1,000 replicates. The flat XGBoost model achieved a macro AUROC of 0.832 in development, 0.789 on temporal evaluation, and 0.739 to 0.782 across external cohorts. Across 25 algorithm-by-setting comparisons, the cascade never significantly outperformed the flat model except for the decision tree, and absolute differences were below 0.02 in 23 comparisons. Prolonged-stay AUPRC declined from 0.303 in development to 0.143-0.234 externally, tracking class prevalence. A death-first cascade provided no consistent advantage over a simpler flat multiclass model. Transportability and prevalence shift, rather than architectural complexity, were the dominant limitations.

Methods at a glance

What was compared?

Direct multiclass: one model outputs early discharge, in-hospital death, and prolonged hospitalization.

Death-first two-stage: stage 1 predicts death (pD); stage 2 predicts prolonged hospitalization conditional on non-death (q).

Final three-class probabilities
Early: (1 − pD)(1 − q)
Death: pD
Prolonged: (1 − pD)q

Prediction landmark: 24 hours after ICU admission. The study protocol defines 28-day outcomes from hospital admission.

Main finding

Clinical hierarchy did not consistently improve discrimination

Across algorithmsOnly 1 of 25 comparisons nominally favored two-stage prediction.
XGBoostMulticlass estimates were numerically higher in all four external cohorts.
InterpretationThe observed differences do not establish statistical equivalence.

Nominal significance: unadjusted 95% confidence interval for paired Δ macro AUROC excludes zero; 1,000 patient-level paired bootstrap resamples. No multiple-comparison correction was applied.

Interpretation & limitations

Important qualifications

These comparisons are exploratory, and nominal confidence intervals were not adjusted for multiplicity. Non-significant differences do not establish equivalence between prediction structures. Macro AUROC may conceal individual-outcome differences.

Provenance: Cohort inclusion and original Sepsis-3 onset extraction cannot be independently reconstructed in full from the frozen aggregate results. SICdb boundary classifications follow the frozen study labels.
