Prediction of 28-Day Hospital Outcomes in Patients Meeting Sepsis-3 Criteria: A Comparison of Multiclass and Two-Stage Models

Jiheon Kim1, Jihye Shin2, Chaeyeong Yi2, Hyeon Ji Kim2, Bongjo Kim2, Minkook Son2

1 Department of AI, Dong-A University, Busan, Republic of Korea

2 Department of Physiology, Dong-A University College of Medicine, Busan, Republic of Korea

Presentation: KOSMI Fall Conference 2026Poster: P1-07Document type: Conference abstract and supplementary research materials

Abstract

Background: Most sepsis prognostic models focus on mortality, although survivors may experience different hospital outcomes.

Objective: To compare direct multiclass classification with a death-first two-stage approach for predicting 28-day hospital outcomes across temporal and external ICU evaluations.

Methods: Adults meeting Sepsis-3 criteria in MIMIC-IV were used for model development (2008–2016; n = 17,009) and temporal evaluation (2017–2022; n = 5,732). Selected models were refit on the full MIMIC-IV cohort and externally evaluated in two eICU-CRD populations, SICdb, and NWICU. The three outcomes were early discharge alive, in-hospital death, and prolonged hospitalization at day 28. Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM were evaluated using both prediction structures. The primary metric was macro area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), with between-structure differences estimated using 1,000 patient-level paired bootstrap resamples.

Results: The multiclass XGBoost model achieved a development macro AUROC of 0.832, 0.789 in temporal evaluation, and 0.739–0.782 in external evaluation. Across 25 comparisons (five algorithms × five evaluation cohorts), the two-stage approach had a nominally significant advantage in one comparison, while 23 comparisons had an absolute macro AUROC difference below 0.02. Differences were assessed using unadjusted 95% bootstrap confidence intervals; no multiple-comparison correction was applied.

Conclusions: The death-first two-stage approach did not consistently improve discrimination over direct multiclass classification. Direct multiclass classification may provide a simpler reference approach for predicting 28-day hospital outcomes. Further evaluation of calibration and transportability across ICU databases is warranted. These findings do not establish equivalence between the structures.

Keywords: Machine learning; Multiclass classification; External evaluation; Sepsis-3
