Study abstract

Most sepsis prognostic models focus on mortality, although survivors may have different hospital outcomes. We compared a multiclass model with a death-first two-stage model for predicting 28-day hospital outcomes.

Adults meeting Sepsis-3 criteria in MIMIC-IV were used for model development (2008–2016, n=17,009) and temporal evaluation (2017–2022, n=5,732). The selected models were subsequently refit on the full MIMIC-IV dataset and externally evaluated in two eICU-CRD populations, SICdb, and NWICU. Outcomes were early discharge, in-hospital death, and prolonged hospitalization at day 28. Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM were evaluated under both structures. Macro AUROC was the primary metric; differences were estimated using 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples.

XGBoost achieved the highest development macro AUROC (0.832), with 0.789 in temporal evaluation and 0.739–0.782 in external evaluation. Across 25 comparisons, the two-stage model significantly outperformed the multiclass model only once; absolute differences were <0.02 in 23 comparisons.

The death-first two-stage model did not provide a consistent discrimination benefit over direct multiclass classification. These findings support the multiclass model as a parsimonious baseline, while further evaluation of calibration and transportability across ICU databases is warranted.

Study design

When were predictions made?

  1. Hospital admissionThe 28-day outcome window begins.
  2. 24 hours after ICU admissionThe models use information available by this landmark. Patients who died or were discharged before the landmark were excluded.
  3. Day 28 after hospital admissionEach patient has one outcome: discharge alive before day 28, in-hospital death by day 28, or continued hospitalization at day 28. A patient still in hospital on day 28 who dies later is in the continued-hospitalization group.

How the two structures predict

Direct multiclass classification estimates the three outcome probabilities together. The two-stage model estimates death first, then the probability of continued hospitalization conditional on survival.

Which 56 predictors were used?

The first-day feature set excluded APS III. Each listed minimum and maximum counts as a separate input.

Demographics and admission · 11
Age, male sex, five recorded race indicators (White, Black, Hispanic, Asian, other), three admission-type indicators (emergency, elective, other), and length of stay before ICU admission.
Clinical status and support · 9
Charlson Comorbidity Index, Glasgow Coma Scale, body mass index, urine output, continuous renal replacement therapy, vasopressin, epinephrine, norepinephrine, and invasive mechanical ventilation.
Vital signs · 14
Minimum and maximum heart rate, systolic pressure, diastolic pressure, mean blood pressure, respiratory rate, temperature, and oxygen saturation.
Laboratory values · 22
Minimum and maximum blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, white blood cell count, hemoglobin, platelet count, chloride, sodium, bicarbonate, potassium, glucose, and calcium.

Development and evaluation

MIMIC-IV development used 17,009 patients from 2008–2016. Models selected in development were evaluated on 5,732 later MIMIC-IV patients and refit on the full MIMIC-IV cohort for external evaluation. The four external cohorts comprise two eICU-CRD definitions, SICdb, and NWICU. The two eICU cohorts share 1,369 patients.
